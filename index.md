# A Reader's Guide to LiveLore: Horror Stories Where the Audience Votes


Campfire stories always worked because the listeners could interrupt. [LiveLore](https://livelore.ai/) brings a little of that back to reading: free scary stories, some of them continued by reader ballots. This guide explains what the site offers, who it suits and how to get the most out of it.

## What LiveLore offers, item by item

1. **Free stories, no sign-up.** Complete tales and clearly labeled chapters can be read without an account.
2. **Reader-choice chapters.** Some serials end a chapter with a ballot. Readers choose where the plot goes, and the next chapter follows the winning option. Ballot dates are published, and if nobody votes the story continues under an announced editorial fallback.
3. **Noctur, a weekly horror slot.** [Noctur](https://livelore.ai/noctur/) publishes a new chapter every Wednesday at 8 p.m. New York time.
4. **An interactive mystery.** [The Last Collection](https://livelore.ai/stories/the-last-collection/) is a non-supernatural mystery about a missing museum letter, where readers decide how the heroine exposes the theft.
5. **Read-aloud tales.** The [campfire scary stories](https://livelore.ai/stories/campfire-scary-stories/) are five short pieces, 3 to 4 minutes each, written to be told out loud.
6. **Audio narration.** Selected stories include AI narration with music, labeled as such.
7. **Transparent labeling.** Stories are marked as AI-assisted and human-reviewed, with content notes on themes.

## Use cases

- **A camping trip or sleepover** that needs a five-minute story someone can read from a phone.
- **A weekly ritual** for readers who like serialized fiction and want a fixed release night.
- **Classroom or club reading** where the group debates the ballot together before voting.
- **Aspiring horror writers** studying structure; the site's [horror writing guide](https://livelore.ai/how-to-write-a-horror-story/) covers the basics.

## Needs it meets

- **"I want something short tonight."** Lists of short and one-sentence stories are built for exactly that.
- **"I want a say in the story."** Ballots give readers real influence over what happens next.
- **"I want to know what I'm reading."** Content notes and AI-assisted labels set expectations up front.
- **"I don't want another account."** Reading is open; no login is needed.

## How to start

Pick by mood. For a group, open the campfire collection and take turns reading. For a slow burn, start Noctur's current serial and set a reminder for Wednesday. For a puzzle rather than a scare, begin The Last Collection and cast your vote before the ballot closes.
